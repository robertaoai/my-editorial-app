RISK 01 / 05
📖 Four-Eyes Independence (Line Separation)
Feynman definition
Risky work needs two different pairs of eyes on it before it goes out — one pair that does the work, another that checks it — and those two pairs must belong to genuinely different parties, never the same party wearing two hats. Here that's enforced by which Line of the Three Lines Model executed a step, not by which literal agent instance ran it.

Immutable business rules
The same person or agent may never hold both a Line 1 and a Line 2 role. Re-sourced 2026-08-30 (D-163), corrected 2026-08-31 (D-165): this is a hard rule from the project's own editorial RACI — not an IIA Three Lines Model citation — but the rule is successor-node separation across the boundary, R(prior task) ≠ R(this task) (e.g. R(T4) ≠ R(T5)), not "R never equals A within one task." At T5, R = A by design — the Acting Chief Editor is both — and that is the expected shape, not a violation; four-eyes holds because R(T4), the Journalist agent, differs from R(T5). D-163's original register rationale stated the rule backwards on its own cited source; D-165 corrected it after Lane B verified it directly against the RACI matrix. The Three Lines Model still governs a separate, real concern (organizational assurance, Line 3), split into its own requirement SEC-06 — now anchored to the 2024 Global Internal Audit Standards (issued 9 Jan 2024, effective 9 Jan 2025), not a "2026 Statement of Position" that does not exist as a distinct edition (D-166). Modular_PRD §6.5 SEC-01/SEC-06 · docs/governance/raci-involvement-matrix.md §2, §4
Four-eyes applies only at Line-boundary crossings — T5 (1→2) and T6 (2→1). Within a Line, the same agent may run adjacent gates; that is the standard rule, not an exception. Blueprint §4.7
T5 must be executed by a human. An agent that attempts it is refused outright — this is AC-06, named in the fn-spec as "the canary" for the whole feature. FN-GATES-01-05 §3.4
Every transition classifies into exactly three outcomes — satisfied, not_applicable, override_not_four_eyes — never a silent fourth state. FR-05, Modular_PRD §5
An override is permitted but must be logged as an override (event_type = HumanOverride) — never silently recorded as compliant.
Emergency bypass skips the independence check only — never phase gates, required fields, audit logging, or publication confirmation.
The Line 2 reviewer records a disposition before seeing the Line 1 recommendation — the blind first pass. This ordering is the requirement: without it, the one pre-launch detector this concept has (G-05) cannot distinguish real agreement from anchoring. FN-GATES-01-05 §3.4
Data & system boundaries
Feeds from workflow_transitions.line_separation_status (renamed from judgment_independence_status by decision D-111), plus line_assignment, identity_assurance (self_asserted | authenticated), and assisting_agent_id.

Computed purely by comparing the executor's Line to the gate's required Line — a structural fact. It does not and cannot answer whether the agent actually reasoned independently; that cognitive question is OD2, and it stays genuinely Open at Charter level regardless of what this field reads.

RISK 02 / 05
📖 Resolved vs. Ratified
Feynman definition
"Resolved" means someone found a defensible answer to an open question. "Ratified" means the one person with real authority — the Chief Editor — formally signed off on that answer, on a specific date, in a specific place. A resolved-but-unratified decision may be built against provisionally; it may not be reported as settled.

Immutable business rules
A structural resolution (e.g. "OD1 is resolved by the Three Lines Model mapping") is a model of a decision, not the decision itself. Addendum §11
Ratification is a discrete, dated Chief Editor act, recorded with an approval-artifact reference in the Addendum §2.4 Ratification Log — never inferred from age, repetition, or silence.
No document other than that log may ratify anything. Modular_PRD §0.2 — "a decision recorded only in this PRD is not a decision"
OD1–OD3 remain Open at Charter level even after Three Lines Model resolution and Entry 007's Phase-0 acceptance. "Provisional-for-PoC" is a third state — distinct from both unratified and ratified.
Every OD-dependent requirement carries its own ⚠ marker naming the OD; an ordinary (non-Charter) decision like Q11 carries a different, non-interchangeable marker. A doubled ⚠⚠ is never valid. Modular_PRD §0.4
Data & system boundaries
Lives entirely in the Addendum §2.4 Ratification Log table — columns Resolved?, Ratified?, Ratified Date, Approval Artifact. The Provisional Deviation Register can close a deviation-from-Charter-text row without that act being a ratification of anything — closing and ratifying are different verbs with different owners.

Success drift baseline
Now
A standing per-sprint ritual: re-check every ⚠ marker — has its OD actually moved? Output is "no silent upgrade from provisional to settled." Modular_PRD §12

Drift signal — already fired five times
D1–D4 once relabelled "Ratified" without sign-off.
OD3 called "Resolved" in two separate document versions.
The independence success criterion was silently upgraded to "unconditional" and survived three review passes — it hid inside a success-criterion sentence rather than a status table.
The corpus names its own likeliest next occurrence: a requirement's own Outputs line, "because FRs read as commitments and qualifiers look like clutter."
RISK 03 / 05
📖 Newsworthiness vs. Trend Score vs. Editorial Priority
Feynman definition
Three different questions get asked about the same article, and they must never collapse into one number: Trend Score — is this popular right now? Editorial Priority — does this fit our strategy and audience? And Newsworthiness — is this worth chasing and safe to pursue at all, independent of whether it's popular?

Immutable business rules
Trend Score and Editorial Priority are separate v0 formulas with separate weights, each independently visible on the article card — never merged into one field. Blueprint §11.3
Newsworthiness is not truth, and popularity is not safety: "popular but unreliable content scores high on trend and fails verification" is a named required test case. SOP Fallback Plan §Phase P3
No confidence or trend score may ever auto-advance a gate — a fixed non-goal (NG-10), because every gate has a named executor and auto-advancing past one is a bypass by definition.
The v0 formula weights are explicit unvalidated starting assumptions — reviewable only after 50+ scored articles, never treated as tuned.
CR-06 (trend signals) is not computable on the shipped schema today (TC3) — a disclosed gap (FB-06), not a silent omission.
Data & system boundaries
Trend Score reads source_published_date, source.reliability_tier, engagement_metrics, ShareVelocity. Editorial Priority reads topic-lineage depth, reviewer confidence, source/topic authority match, category balance, evidence quality.

A proposed Newsworthiness Profile — audience fit, timeliness, impact, novelty, source authority, corroboration, mission fit, each independently visible with a rationale — exists only in docs/governance/media-industry-sop-fallback-implementation-plan.md §6.1 and has not propagated into Modular_PRD.md. None of the three may write back to current_state.

Success drift baseline
Now
display_priority is a dashboard sort order only. NG-10 holds. CR-06's honest status is disclosed, not hidden.

Drift signal — live and unresolved
A high trend score read as evidence a story is verified — the corpus's own named "guaranteed failure mode."
The three terms used interchangeably in a future spec or conversation.
Ruled 2026-08-28 — CR-06's scoring is backlog, not a v1 gap. FB-06 ("not deliverable in v1", blocked by TC3) is dispositioned to Modular_PRD §2.5.1's backlog-refinement path, the same treatment as CR-14's deferred inference half. It needs a "Ready when" row — a deferral with no return path is the defect D-148 built that section to prevent. The SOP Fallback Plan's competing "promote to core Product Scope" framing (AF-03) is superseded by this disposition.
RISK 04 / 05
📖 Article State vs. Publication Status
Feynman definition
An article's path through the newsroom (Discovered → … → Published) is one clock. Whether it actually made it out to WordPress, or LinkedIn, or a guest platform is a separate clock — one per destination — because an article can succeed on one platform while failing on another at the same instant.

Immutable business rules
ManualReady is a PUBLICATION.status value, per target — never an ARTICLE.current_state value. Two enums, two entities. Blueprint §4.5
current_state becomes Published the moment at least one target holds a live URL — not when every target does.
MockPublished is fixed and non-configurable, and never satisfies Published — "what stops demo data becoming false evidence." It now has a second legitimate trigger (a completed M-POC evidence-review engagement) sharing the same enum value, asserted to need no schema change. FN-PUBLICATION §3.3.2 · FN-POC-ENGAGEMENT §4.4
publications rows are append-only status events, never a mutable status column — overwriting in place would destroy retry and partial-failure history. D-114
Partial success across targets is the expected shape, not an error condition.
Data & system boundaries
ARTICLE.current_state — ten-value enum, one value per article. PUBLICATION.status — seven values (Pending, Published, Failed, Scheduled, Cancelled, ManualReady, MockPublished), one row-history per article × target. The article-level "Published" reading is always a read-time derivation over the publications table — the publish function never writes it directly. SPECS-PUBLICATION §3

Success drift baseline
Now
AC-15's target state: WordPress Published and LinkedIn ManualReady held simultaneously, article reads Published. (Currently disclosed as not yet representable — TC2, S1 work.)

Drift signal
Any code path setting article.current_state directly from inside the publish job instead of deriving it.
A metric that treats a MockPublished target as evidence of real reach.
The two distinct reasons for MockPublished — test mode vs. a paid client engagement — silently merging in a future report.
RISK 05 / 05
📖 The Independent-Assurance Cluster
Successor-node review · Line 3 · OD4

Feynman definition
Three different "someone else double-checks this" mechanisms exist, and they are not interchangeable. The next gate routinely re-checks the previous gate's work as a side effect of doing its own job (always on, free). A special outside auditor, Line 3, is called in only when risk crosses a threshold (occasional, independent). And a possible future system, OD4 ("Proposer → Critics → Judge"), is not a reviewer of other gates at all — it is a smarter way to produce one gate's own answer, which then still passes through the ordinary chain.

Immutable business rules
Successor-node review is already specified, not a new proposal — it is implicit in each gate's required-fields list. T6's required field is literally "all prior gate criteria confirmed," meaning T6 already reviews T5. RACI matrix §2.1
Because of that, Line 3 is additional risk-triggered assurance over the whole chain — never "the only control" covering a task where Responsible = Accountable. An earlier draft claimed exactly that and was explicitly withdrawn once found. RACI matrix changelog v1.1
Line 3 must share no data path and no reporting line back into Line 1 or Line 2's self-assessment — "if it did, it would stop being Line 3 and become a rubber stamp." (Stated near-verbatim in four separate documents.)
OD4 means exactly Proposer → Critics → Judge — never a synonym for house SOP, rule-version control, Chief Editor authority, or ordinary phase-gate review. An earlier governance draft used it to mean judgment-rule governance generally; this was found to be a category error and corrected. poc-feedback-approval-crosswalk §7.4
OD4 is intra-node (changes how one gate reaches its answer); the phase gates are inter-node (review the handoff between gates). Adopting OD4 never replaces or weakens the gate chain. RACI matrix §6.1
The system applying a judgment rule may never be the sole authority approving the rule governing itself — rule governance sits outside both the ordinary pipeline and any future OD4 system.
Corrected 2026-08-31 (D-167): Line 3's build status is governed by the Three Lines Model's OD4 branch ② (the Charter's pre-launch-blocker branch, triggered if OD2 resolves negatively) — never OD3, which is the Charter's separate, still-open question of agent headcount and roster shape and was never about whether Line 3 exists. Q2 ("supply a Line 3 executor, or state Line 3 is human/external for v1") was answered on 2026-08-20 (D-57): v1 discloses no independent assurance; FR-11 is not built in v1, conditional on C-13's business-continuity surface as a compensating control, never Line 3 itself. Six-plus current-value locations in the governed corpus still called this "open" or "blocked" after D-57 decided it — the same staleness class as Entry 01's own drift, at wider scope.
Data & system boundaries
Successor review carries no field of its own — it's implicit in each gate's required-fields list. Line 3 findings: event_type = Line3Audit, line_assignment = Line3; triggers are risk-threshold, novel/controversial flag, emergency-bypass-used, 3+ repeated returns, or HumanOverride-used. OD4: no schema exists; would require a wholly separate, newly-chartered portfolio project (AP-OD4) if its trigger ever fires.

Success drift baseline
Now
The falsifiable metric that replaced two untestable framings ("do agents think alike?", "is the human diligent?"): the T6→T5 return rate. A rate that never leaves zero means successor review is nominal at that boundary — RACI-03. Line 3 itself: decided, not open — no independent assurance in v1 (D-57, D-167), C-13's BCP surface standing in as the disclosed compensating control, never as Line 3.

Drift signal
"OD4" used loosely for "the rule-approval process" or "an AI that checks other AI" generally — already happened once, already caught.
Treating Line 3 as the sole safety net for a Responsible = Accountable gate, forgetting successor review already covers it — also already happened once.
Wiring a hypothetical OD4 output directly into publication, skipping the ordinary successor chain.
OD3 (headcount/roster) cited as the reason Line 3 has no executor, when the governing decision is OD4 branch ② and Q2 is already answered (D-57) — happened across six-plus locations in the governed corpus, caught and corrected D-167.

RISK 06 / 06 · ruled this pass
📖 CR-14 — Reporter-Gate Tagging (the gap that mostly wasn't)
Feynman definition
When an article first enters the pipeline, somebody has to say what it is about, where it came from, and whether it is trending. The customer asked for that in one line. The corpus then recorded it as completely unbuilt for months — but the tagging step was specified all along, at the highest tier there is. What was never specified is how clever the tagging has to be: no accuracy target, no extraction method, no automation threshold. The step is v1. The cleverness is not.

The ruling — Chief Editor, 2026-08-28
1. CR-14 never specified automation depth. It names a gate and an executor. It sets no accuracy target, no inference mechanism, and no threshold at which tagging counts as done.

2. The already-specified tagging path therefore satisfies it. The frozen Charter settles the executor: "Tagging is agent-executed at the Reporter stage; the Chief Editor is the sole human reviewer, not the sole tagger." Blueprint §2.3 repeats it and AT-075 tests it.

3. Q9 answered — the tag itself is v1; everything past it is backlog. US-01 already carries the flow, so the tag is in scope. Automated inference quality is out, and returns through Modular_PRD §2.5.1's backlog path like any other deferred item.

One precision on wording: read "manual" as without automated inference, not as human-tagged. The Charter forbids the second reading — the agent applies the tags either way; only the sophistication behind them changes.
